# Personal Dictionary

Words and expressions I want to remember. Entries use the base form where possible; examples are included when they help show usage.

## A

- **accommodate** — provide space for someone or adapt to their needs.
- **accumulate** — gather or build up over time.
- **acronym** — a word formed from the initial letters of a name or phrase.
- **aggregation** — the process of gathering items into a group or whole.
- **allocate** — assign or distribute something for a particular purpose; Bengali: বরাদ্দ করা.
- **analogy** (*plural: analogies*) — a comparison used to explain how two things are similar.
- **anticipate** — expect or prepare for something before it happens.
- **aspect** — one part or feature of something.
- **assertion** — a confident statement or claim.
- **assessment** — an evaluation or estimate.
- **attribute** — as a noun, a quality or feature; as a verb, to regard something as caused by something else.

## B

- **bastion** — a stronghold; figuratively, a person or institution that strongly supports or protects something.
- **BTW** — informal abbreviation for “by the way”; Bengali equivalents include প্রসঙ্গত and যাই হোক.

## C

- **catastrophic** — causing sudden, very great damage or suffering.
- **characteristic** — a typical quality or feature.
- **chore** — a routine or necessary task.
- **circular** — shaped like a circle; also, a notice sent to a group of people.
- **collision** — an impact between moving objects; also, a clash between ideas or interests.
- **commence** — begin.
- **compliance** — the act of following a rule, request, or requirement.
- **composite** — made up of multiple parts or materials.
- **comprehensive** — broad and complete; covering many or all relevant parts.
- **comprise** — consist of or be made up of. In careful usage, the whole comprises its parts.
- **concise** — brief but clear.
- **congenital** — present from birth; it is not necessarily inherited.
- **consecutive** — following one after another without interruption.
- **consist** — be made up of; commonly used as “consist of.”
- **consolidate** — combine things into a single, stronger, or more manageable whole.
- **consolidation** — the process of combining or strengthening things into a whole.
- **constitute** — make up or form a whole; also, amount to or be considered something.
- **constraint** — a limit or restriction.
- **contiguous** — sharing a border or touching; adjacent.
- **contingency** — a possible future event or condition, especially one that requires preparation.
- **convey** — communicate or carry something from one place or person to another.
- **credibility** — the quality of being trusted or believed.
- **cumbersome** — difficult to handle or use because it is large, heavy, or complicated.

## D

- **dazed** — stunned or confused, and temporarily unable to think clearly.
- **decouple** — separate things that were connected or dependent on each other.
- **deduce** — reach a conclusion by reasoning from evidence or facts.
- **deduplication** — removing duplicate copies of data or items.
- **delegate** — as a noun, a person chosen to represent others; as a verb, entrust a task or authority to someone else.
- **denote** — indicate or represent something.
- **derive** — obtain or develop something from a source.
- **descendant** — a person, animal, or thing that comes from an earlier one; a later generation.
- **despise** — feel strong dislike or contempt for.
- **disinter** — literally, remove something from a grave; figuratively, bring something hidden or forgotten to light.
- **distinct** — clearly different or recognizable.

## E

- **elicit** — draw out a response, information, or reaction. *The question elicited a detailed answer.*
- **eloquent** — fluent and persuasive in speaking or writing.
- **enthusiast** — a person who is very interested in a particular activity or subject.
- **entity** — something that exists as a distinct unit, such as an organisation or object.
- **enumerate** — list items one by one.
- **ephemeral** — lasting for a very short time.
- **epilogue** — a concluding section at the end of a book, play, or other work.
- **erroneous** — mistaken or incorrect.
- **escalate** — increase in intensity, seriousness, or scale.
- **et cetera (etc.)** — “and other similar things”; used to show that a list continues.
- **expedite** — make a process happen sooner or more quickly.
- **explicitly** — in a direct and clearly stated way.

## F

- **feasible** — practical and possible to do; not simply probable.

## G

- **generic** — shared by a whole class or type rather than specific to one example; also, not tied to a particular brand.
- **granular** — organized or described in fine detail; literally, made of small grains.
- **grief** — deep sorrow, especially after a loss.

## H

- **homogeneous** — uniform or made up of similar parts.
- **hypothetical** — based on an imagined situation or assumption, rather than presented as fact.

## I

- **i.e. (id est)** — “that is”; introduces a clarification or restatement.
- **immensely** — to a very great extent; very much.
- **immerse** — involve deeply in an activity, or submerge in a liquid.
- **immersion** — deep involvement in an activity or subject; also, the act of submerging something.
- **imperative** — essential or urgent; as a noun, an instruction or command.
- **implicitly** — indirectly suggested or understood without being stated outright.
- **inbound** — coming into a place, system, or organisation.
- **incarnation** — a person or form that embodies an idea or quality; not a statue.
- **inclusion** — the act of including or adding someone or something.
- **incorporate** — include something as part of a larger whole.
- **index** (*plural: indexes or indices*) — an alphabetical list or guide; in mathematics and computing, a value or position used to identify an item.
- **inevitably** — in a way that cannot be avoided or prevented.
- **infrastructure** — the basic systems and facilities that allow an organisation or society to operate.
- **ingestion** — the act of taking something in, such as food or data.
- **intermittent** — starting and stopping at intervals; not continuous.
- **introspective** — examining your own thoughts and feelings.
- **intrusion** — an unwelcome entry, interruption, or involvement.
- **inventory** — a detailed list of items, goods, or resources.

## J

- **judicious** — showing good judgment and careful thought.

## L

- **latter** — the second of two things just mentioned.
- **linear** — arranged along a line or in a straight sequence; in some contexts, changing at a constant rate.
- **literal** — using the exact, ordinary meaning of words rather than a figurative meaning.

## M

- **magnitude** — the size, extent, or importance of something.
- **metaphor** — a figure of speech that describes one thing as another to suggest a similarity.
- **misnomer** — an inaccurate or unsuitable name for something.
- **mitigation** — action that reduces the severity or impact of a problem.

## N

- **neat** — tidy, orderly, or arranged carefully.
- **notion** — an idea, belief, or impression.

## O

- **obscure** — not clear or well known; also, to make something difficult to see or understand.
- **opt** — choose one option, often from several.
- **orchestration** — the coordination of several parts or activities to achieve a result.
- **outbound** — going out from a place, system, or organisation.

## P

- **paradigm** — a model or example that shapes how something is understood or done.
- **parity** — equality or a comparable state.
- **permutation** — one possible arrangement or ordering of a set of items.
- **persistent** — continuing over time or despite difficulty; not the same as static.
- **precedent** — an earlier example or decision used as a guide for later cases.
- **preface** — an introduction at the beginning of a book or document.
- **preserve** — keep something safe or maintain it in its existing condition.
- **proliferate** — increase or spread rapidly.
- **prone** — likely or susceptible to experience or do something, often something undesirable; commonly used as “prone to.”
- **propagate** — spread or cause something to spread; also, breed or grow plants.
- **prospective** — expected or possible in the future; for example, a prospective customer is a potential customer.
- **provision** — the act of supplying something; also, a condition or clause in an agreement or law.

## Q

- **queue** — a line or sequence of people or items waiting to be served or processed.

## R

- **rational** — based on reason or logic.
- **redact** — remove or obscure sensitive information from a document before sharing or publishing it.
- **regulation** — an official rule made by an authority.
- **remediation** — action taken to correct a problem or reduce its effects, including environmental damage.
- **resemble** — look like or be similar to.
- **resilient** — able to recover quickly or withstand difficulty; not rigid or stiff.
- **respite** — a short period of rest or relief.
- **restraint** — self-control or a limit that prevents an action.
- **retention** — the act or ability of keeping or holding something.

## S

- **scaffolding** — a temporary structure that supports workers or materials; figuratively, temporary support that helps someone learn or complete a task.
- **scarce** — in short supply; Bengali: দুষ্প্রাপ্য.
- **scummy** — informal and strongly negative; dirty, unpleasant, or morally objectionable.
- **simulate** — imitate the behaviour or appearance of something, often for study or training.
- **sophistication** — complexity, refinement, or advanced development; the intended sense depends on context.
- **stale** — no longer fresh or current; can describe food, air, or outdated information.
- **stash** — store or hide something for future use.
- **stimulate** — encourage activity, growth, interest, or a response.
- **strive** — make a great effort to achieve something.
- **strung** — past tense or past participle of “string”; for example, lights can be strung across a room.
- **subsequent** — coming after something else.
- **substantial** — large or significant in amount, size, or importance.

## T

- **tactic** — a planned action used to achieve a particular result.
- **tedious** — long, repetitive, and boring.
- **threshold** — a level or point at which something begins or changes; literally, the strip at the bottom of a doorway.
- **thus** — therefore; or, in some contexts, in this way.
- **traverse** — travel or move across or through something.

## U

- **ubiquitous** — present or found everywhere.
- **ubiquity** — the state of being present or found everywhere; Bengali: সর্বব্যাপীতা.

## V

- **versatile** — able to adapt to different tasks or uses.
- **vicinity** — the area near a particular place; neighbourhood.

## W

- **whim** — a sudden, often unplanned desire or idea.
- **wicked** — morally bad or evil; informally, can mean excellent in some varieties of English.
- **wondrous** — inspiring wonder; remarkable or extraordinary.
